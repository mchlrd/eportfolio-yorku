Goal 01

Discover myself.

Why this goal comes first

Before I can argue that I'm ready for a career, I need to be honest about what I actually want, what I'm genuinely good at, and where my judgement is weakest. The four portfolios below are that audit — written down, so it can be checked against reality later.

Artifact · Written reflection

Why computer science — and why security

A short account of how I got here: the early curiosity, the decision to study Computer Science at Lassonde, and the moment security became the thing I cared about rather than just a topic I passed.

Life-Long Learning Professionalism
Retell
I took things apart before I understood what they did — usually with the vague intention of putting them back together. That instinct survived into high school and turned into an interest in how systems fit together rather than how they're used. I chose Computer Science at Lassonde because it was the only program where I could study the machinery underneath everything. Security became the focus later, during an internship where I watched an incident get investigated with real consequences attached.
Relate
COOP 2100 frames professional development as something you build deliberately rather than something that happens to you. This artifact is the "why" underneath every other entry in the portfolio — the claim that my interest is genuine and not just a response to what seemed employable. The Life-Long Learning competency is the connecting thread: the reason I keep choosing difficult, unfamiliar material is that I actually find the process satisfying.
Reflect
Writing this down forced me to separate two things I'd been conflating: wanting to be good at something, and wanting to do it. What changed my thinking was noticing that my strongest memories are all of problems I couldn't solve at first. Going forward, I want to make choices based on where the hard problems are, not where the comfortable ones are — and to revisit this artifact each year to check whether that's actually what I'm doing.
Competency justification This artifact demonstrates Life-Long Learning because it documents a pattern of deliberately pursuing material beyond what was required — moving from general computing to security because it was harder, not because it was easier — and it establishes the self-directed standard the rest of the portfolio is measured against.

Three SMART goals for the next 12 months

Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound goals derived from my assessment results — each one producing something that can be shown, not just claimed.

Life-Long Learning Professionalism

Goal 1 · Target: April 2027

Specific · Measurable

Earn a cloud security certification

Complete the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner exam, then begin Solutions Architect – Associate study. Measurable: pass the exam and add the credential ID to Section B of this portfolio. Relevant: directly supports the cloud-security direction in my career goal. Achievable: builds on the AWS work already done during my placement.

Goal 2 · Target: August 2027

Specific · Time-bound

Build a documented detection lab

Stand up a home lab with Sysmon and a SIEM, simulate three attack techniques, and publish three written detections with the queries and the reasoning behind them. Measurable: three public write-ups linked from Section E. Relevant: closes the gap between operating tools and explaining detections.

Goal 3 · Target: ongoing

Measurable · Relevant

Practise explaining technical work to non-technical people

Present one technical topic per term to a non-specialist audience and collect feedback each time. Measurable: one presentation per term with written feedback retained. Relevant: addresses my weakest assessed area and the communication expectations of a professional engineering role.

Retell
I reviewed my interest and skills assessment results and cross-checked them against what I've actually produced — code, reports, and working systems. Where the two agreed I kept the claim; where the assessment flattered me, I moved it to a development area.
Relate
The course treats goal-setting as a skill with a structure, and SMART is that structure: vague intentions ("get better at cloud") are unfalsifiable, while "pass this exam by this date and post the credential" can be checked by anyone reading this page. This is the Life-Long Learning competency made auditable — the portfolio becomes the evidence record rather than a summary of intentions.
Reflect
The uncomfortable part was Goal 3. My assessments were strongest where I already had proof (automation, analysis) and weakest at exactly the thing that decides whether good work is ever adopted: explaining it to someone who doesn't share my vocabulary. I've been treating communication as a soft skill to pick up later. Writing it as a measurable goal with feedback attached was the moment it became something I'd actually be held to — and the thing I'd do differently is start collecting that feedback now rather than after graduation.
Competency justification This artifact demonstrates Life-Long Learning by converting self-assessment into measurable, dated development goals with defined evidence, and Professionalism by naming a genuine weakness and committing to a process for correcting it.

Artifact · EQ & learning style reflection

Applying my EQ and learning style in a real workplace

A reflection on how my personality and learning preferences played out during a placement — including where they helped and where they actively worked against me.

Individual & Teamwork Professionalism Ethics & Equity
Retell
During my placement I worked across an operations team where priorities changed weekly. My natural approach — pick up the problem, go quiet, come back with a working answer — produced good results but made me hard to track. When a colleague asked for a status update I didn't have one that fit into a sentence, because I'd been holding the whole problem in my head.
Relate
The course's EQ material separates self-awareness from self-management, which is the distinction that mattered here: I was aware that I go heads-down, but I hadn't managed it as a team behaviour. This artifact connects to Individual & Teamwork because working well alone isn't the same as being easy to work with — the second requires deliberately surfacing progress at a colleague's rhythm rather than my own. It also touches Ethics & Equity in the values I listed: deciding what happens to people affected by a system, when they're not the ones asking for it.
Reflect
What I learned about myself is that my independence is a strength with a real cost attached: it optimises for the quality of my output and quietly degrades other people's ability to plan around me. What changed my thinking was realising that a status update isn't administrative overhead — it's how a team allocates trust. What I'd do differently is write the two-line update before I start solving, not after, and say "I don't know yet, and here's when I will" instead of staying silent until I do.
Competency justification This artifact demonstrates Individual & Teamwork by analysing a concrete collaboration failure and naming the specific behaviour change that addresses it, rather than describing teamwork in the abstract.
