C — Goals & Portfolios

Three goals, each with portfolios gathering evidence of progress

Goals are the organising framework for this portfolio. Each goal below holds one or more portfolios, and each portfolio collects documents, artifacts and reflections that show movement rather than arrival. The sequence starts inward — Discover Myself — and then builds outward into the two career goals I am actually working toward.

## Goal 1 — Discover Myself

Before I can argue that I am ready for a career, I need to be honest about what I want, what I am actually good at, and where my judgement is weakest. The four portfolios below are that audit, written down so it can be checked against reality later.

### Portfolio 1.1 — Dreams and Goals

Contents: where I started, why I chose this field, and what I am aiming at. What I hoped to learn: whether my stated goals are genuinely mine, or just the path of least resistance.

▸ artifact: why computer science — and why security Life-Long Learning

reflection

Retell — what I created or did

I wrote a short account of how I got here: the early curiosity, the decision to study Computer Science at Lassonde, and the point at which security stopped being a subject I was passing and became the thing I cared about.

Relate — how this connects to my goals

This is the "why" underneath every other entry in the portfolio. Professional development is something you build deliberately rather than something that happens to you, and this artifact is the claim that my interest is genuine rather than a response to what seemed employable. It also sets the standard the other portfolios are measured against.

Reflect — what I learned

Writing it down forced me to separate two things I had been conflating: wanting to be good at something, and wanting to do it. What changed my thinking was noticing that my strongest memories are all of problems I could not solve at first. I now want to choose based on where the hard problems are, not where the comfortable ones are — and to re-read this each year to check whether that is actually what I am doing.

Competency

Life-Long Learning — it documents a pattern of deliberately pursuing material beyond what was required, and establishes the self-directed standard the rest of the portfolio is measured against.

reflection

Retell — what I created or did

I cross-checked what I claim about myself against what I have actually produced — code, reports, working systems. Where the two agreed I kept the claim; where the claim ran ahead of the evidence I moved it to a development area. The remaining gaps became three SMART goals rather than three intentions.

Relate — how this connects to my goals

Goal-setting is a skill with a structure, and SMART is that structure. A vague intention like "get better at cloud" cannot be checked by anyone; "pass this exam by this date and post the credential" can. This is what makes the portfolio an evidence record rather than a list of intentions, which is exactly what Goal 1 is meant to establish.

Reflect — what I learned

The uncomfortable part was goal 3. My assessments were strongest where I already had proof and weakest at the thing that decides whether good work is ever adopted: explaining it to someone who does not share my vocabulary. I had been treating communication as a soft skill to pick up later. Writing it as a measurable goal with feedback attached is what turned it into something I will actually be held to. What I would do differently is start collecting that feedback now rather than after graduation.

Competency

Life-Long Learning — converting self-assessment into measurable, dated goals with defined evidence. Professionalism — naming a genuine weakness and committing to a process for correcting it.

reflection

Retell — what I created or did

During my placement I worked across an operations team whose priorities changed weekly. My natural approach — pick up the problem, go quiet, come back with a working answer — produced good results but made me hard to track. When a colleague asked for a status update I did not have one that fit into a sentence, because I had been holding the whole problem in my head.

Relate — how this connects to my goals

The distinction that mattered here is the one between self-awareness and self-management: I was aware that I go heads-down, but I had not managed it as a team behaviour. That connects to my career goals too, because every role I am aiming at involves work that other people have to plan around — incident response especially, where nobody can coordinate around silence.

Reflect — what I learned

What I learned about myself is that my independence is a strength with a real cost: it optimises for the quality of my output and quietly degrades other people's ability to plan around me. What changed my thinking was realising that a status update is not administrative overhead — it is how a team allocates trust. What I would do differently is write the two-line update before I start solving, not after, and say "I do not know yet, and here is when I will" instead of staying silent until I do.

Competency

Individual and Teamwork — it analyses a concrete collaboration failure and names the behaviour change that addresses it, rather than describing teamwork in the abstract.

## Goal 3 — Protect public & critical infrastructure

The longest-term goal, and the one that gives the others a reason. It is about applying technical depth where the consequences are real — services people depend on without ever thinking about them.

### Portfolio 3.1 — Public-sector practice

Contents: what working inside a public institution actually taught me. What I hoped to learn: whether the constraints of public-sector engineering are limitations or the point.

My current placement is my first sustained experience of infrastructure that carries public consequence: identity systems that gate access to services, and vulnerability work where a missed remediation is not a metric but a risk to residents. The most useful thing I have learned is that the hard part is rarely the technology — it is the accountability, the documentation, and the fact that the people affected are not in the room.

### Portfolio 3.2 — Ethics, sustainability and responsibility

Contents: the ethical and sustainability questions my work raises, and how I have reasoned about them. What I hoped to learn: how to hold a technical decision and its consequences in the same thought.
