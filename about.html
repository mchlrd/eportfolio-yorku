Biography

A short introduction.

I'm a Computer Science student at York University's Lassonde School of Engineering with a focus on cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure, and automation. My interest started with a stubborn question — how do you actually prove a system is trustworthy? — and it has since taken me through identity engineering, detection work, and malware analysis inside a live public-sector environment.

Today I work as a Cybersecurity Intern, where most of my value comes from turning repetitive security work into reliable, repeatable automation: identity and access management at scale, vulnerability remediation pipelines, and cloud environments built specifically to be safe to break. I like work that is precise, documented, and genuinely useful to the people who depend on it — and I'd rather ship a small thing that holds up than a large thing that doesn't.

Career goals

Where I'm headed.

Near term

Build depth in security engineering

Finish my co-op terms with a specialist-level command of identity, cloud, and detection engineering, backed by industry certifications rather than claims.

Medium term

Specialise in cloud & infrastructure security

Move from administering environments to designing them: secure-by-default architectures, automated controls, and systems that are hard to misconfigure in the first place.

Long term

Protect public and critical infrastructure

Apply that depth where the consequences are real — the public sector, utilities, and services that people depend on without ever thinking about them.

In one sentence

I want to be an engineer who can both build a system and defend it — someone who understands the infrastructure deeply enough to automate it, and the threat model rigorously enough to anticipate how it fails.
