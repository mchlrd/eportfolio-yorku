D — Reflections

More than what happened

A reflection is not a summary. It is an honest, analytical response to an experience — and every artifact in this portfolio has one attached. They are indexed here so that nothing is buried behind a click.

## Framework

I write each reflection against two frames, because they ask slightly different things and together they force more honesty than either alone. Every reflection below is labelled with all six so a reader can check either one.

Retell
What happened, briefly. → answers what did I create or do?
Relate
Which concepts, frameworks or competencies apply, and why those. → answers how does this connect to my goals?
Reflect
What I learned about myself, and what I would do differently. → answers what did I learn?

Each reflection then closes with an explicit competency justification. Tagging an artifact is not evidence that it demonstrates anything — the argument has to be made in words.

## The standard I hold myself to

Reflections that only Retell demonstrate nothing — they describe activity and leave the reader to guess at the learning. Every reflection here is written so that the Relate and Reflect sections carry the weight, and each one names a specific moment rather than describing a general tendency. Where I could not yet answer honestly — because the experience is still in progress — I have said so rather than inventing a conclusion.

## Critique

An honest note on what this collection does and does not show yet. The reflections are strongest where the evidence is concrete — the placement artifacts, where I can point at a decision I made and a consequence I noticed. They are weaker where I am reasoning about something still in progress, and the two assessment-dependent portfolios in Section C are the thinnest entries until my interest, skills, and EQ results are actually in them. That gap is why the status list on the index page is kept visible rather than tidied away.
